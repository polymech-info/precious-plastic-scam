# High-control groups

A short guide to how they work, and where to read further.

A high-control group organizes people around a mission, a leader, or a moral claim, and then narrows what those people are allowed to know, say, and do. The mission can be religious, political, therapeutic, or practical. The surface story is often idealistic. The working structure is control of information, punishment of dissent, and an uneven split between who benefits and who carries the cost.

This page is an introduction to that structure. The books and papers below are general. They are not a diagnosis of any one person, and citing them does not by itself prove that a named project is a cult. They are useful because the same mechanisms show up across very different groups: loaded language, loyalty tests, a closed story about the outside world, and a rule that leaving or criticizing is a moral failure.

Read for the pattern, not for a label.

- Who controls what members are allowed to read, publish, and ask?
- What happens to someone who disagrees in public?
- Who does the work, who is credited, and who is paid?
- Who keeps the upside when a project succeeds, and who holds the loss when it fails?
- Is disagreement treated as information, or as disloyalty?

## A practical map: the BITE model

Steven Hassan’s BITE model is a plain checklist for coercive control. It sorts the pressure into four kinds. A group does not need all four at full strength. A few, used consistently, are enough to close a room.

- **Behavior.** Time, money, relationships, and daily routine are regulated. Rewards and punishments are public. Leaving has a price.
- **Information.** Outside sources are mocked or banned. Criticism is deleted. Members hear a single account of what the group is and what critics want.
- **Thought.** The group has a private vocabulary. Questions are reframed as selfishness, ignorance, or attack. Doctrine outranks what a person has seen.
- **Emotion.** Fear, guilt, and public shame do the enforcement. Critics are humiliated. People who leave are described as failed, bitter, or dangerous.

Hassan developed the model from his own exit from a high-control group and from later work with people leaving others. A clear interview on how specialists use it is Elizabeth Yuko’s Rolling Stone piece on coercive control, linked at the end.

## 1. Coercive control and ideological manipulation

**Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism**
Robert Jay Lifton, 1961.
[University of North Carolina Press](https://uncpress.org/9780807842539/thought-reform-and-the-psychology-of-totalism/) · [The eight criteria, summarized](https://internationalculticstudies.org/icsa-insights/eight-criteria-for-thought-reform-in-cults/)

Lifton’s study of ideological control names eight pressures: control of communication, demands for purity, a cult of confession, loading the language, doctrine over person, and a sacred science that cannot be wrong. The last two criteria describe who is allowed to exist inside the group and who must be dispensed with.

Use it when a project censors criticism, treats its moral claim as beyond question, and builds an identity that only members are fit to hold.

**Bounded Choice: True Believers and Charismatic Cults**
Janja Lalich, 2004.
[University of California Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520384026/bounded-choice)

Lalich explains how capable people stay inside a closed system. Loyalty and ideology shrink the choices that still feel available. The person is not confused about the mission. The mission has become the frame for every other fact.

Use it when volunteers defend a leadership whose results contradict its promises, and when staying feels like the only decent option.

**Cults in Our Midst**
Margaret Thaler Singer, with Janja Lalich. 1995; revised 2003.
[Wiley](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/humanities-social-sciences/psychology-12ps/social-psychology-12ps4/cults-in-our-midst-978-0-7879-6741-3)

Singer describes coercive persuasion: how people are recruited through an idealistic story, how dependency is built, and how the group answers exit or criticism with punishment.

Use it when recruitment runs on hope, and when money, status, or belonging are withdrawn from anyone who argues.

## 2. Leadership without turning it into a diagnosis

Personality labels travel faster than evidence. The useful question is what the role rewards. These three sources separate a public image from the way power is actually used, and they locate the harm in the fit between a leader, the people around them, and a setting that does not stop them.

**Snakes in Suits: When Psychopaths Go to Work**
Paul Babiak and Robert D. Hare, 2006.

How impression management works inside organizations: charm toward the outside, exploitation of colleagues, and the use of formal power once it is obtained. The public face and the internal record are not the same document.

**The Dark Triad of Personality**
Delroy L. Paulhus and Kevin M. Williams, 2002. *Journal of Research in Personality*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-6566(02)00505-6)

Narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy overlap and still are not the same thing. One is self-aggrandizement, one is strategic manipulation, one is callousness. Collapsing all three into a single insult hides which one is operating.

**The Toxic Triangle: Destructive Leaders, Susceptible Followers, and Conducive Environments**
Art Padilla, Robert Hogan, and Robert B. Kaiser, 2007. *The Leadership Quarterly*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.leaqua.2007.03.001)

Destructive leadership is a triangle, not a personality. It needs a leader who abuses the role, followers who conform or collude, and an environment with weak checks. Moderators who defend the leadership, intimidation treated as normal, and nobody who can force an account: that is the triangle at work.

## 3. Bullying, silence, and exclusion

High-control groups rarely rely on one dramatic punishment. They rely on small, repeated ones: a ban, a pile-on, a person removed from the directory, a question that never gets an answer. The research below is about that ordinary machinery.

**Bullying and Harassment in the Workplace**
Ståle Einarsen, Helge Hoel, Dieter Zapf, and Cary Cooper, editors. 2020.
[Bergen Bullying Research Group](https://www.uib.no/en/rg/bbrg/112056/books)

Sustained harassment under a power imbalance, and what it means when the organization tolerates it. Hostile moderation over months is the subject, not a single rude message.

**The Spiral of Silence**
Elisabeth Noelle-Neumann, 1980.
[University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo3684069.html)

People go quiet when they expect isolation for speaking. A room can look like agreement because the dissenters have already learned the price.

**Ostracism**
Kipling D. Williams, 2007. *Annual Review of Psychology*.
[Review](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.58.110405.085641)

Deliberate exclusion has a measurable effect: loss of belonging, loss of control, and a scramble to get back in. Banning and shunning are not administrative trivia. They are the punishment.

**Moral Disengagement**
Albert Bandura, 1999. *Personality and Social Psychology Review*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0303_3)

How people justify harming others without giving up a moral self-image. The mechanisms include moral justification (“we had to, the mission required it”), diffusion of responsibility, and blaming the person who was harmed. Bandura does not require every participant to share the same intent. A pile-on can run on different motives and still produce the same result.

**Behavioral Study of Obedience**
Stanley Milgram, 1963. *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0040525)

People comply with harm when the instruction comes from someone they treat as a legitimate authority, and when someone else appears to own the responsibility.

**Effects of Group Discussion on Polarization**
David G. Myers and Helmut Lamm, 1975.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1147368/)

Discussion inside a closed group does not average opinions out. It pushes them further in the direction the group already leans, including hostility toward critics.

## 4. When the institution is the harm

**Institutional Betrayal**
Carly Parnitzke Smith and Jennifer J. Freyd, 2014. *American Psychologist*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037564)

What happens when an institution that was supposed to look after people fails them, and then makes it worse: by denying the harm, punishing the report, or protecting the people who caused it. The second injury is the institution’s response.

**Groupthink**
Irving L. Janis, 1971; the book followed in 1972.
[The article](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Dynamics/Janis_Groupthink.pdf)

Cohesive groups suppress dissent, prefer unanimity, and make bad decisions with the contrary evidence sitting in the room. Disagreement is read as disloyalty rather than as a correction.

**Escalation of Commitment**
Barry M. Staw, 1976. *Organizational Behavior and Human Performance*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0030-5073(76)90005-2)

People who own a failing decision often put more resources in rather than admit the failure. The next round of money, time, or reputation is spent defending the last round. This is how a flawed machine, a dead business model, or a broken platform survives on the promise that the next version will fix it.

## 5. Money, green claims, and the respectable setting

Harm in this pattern is often dressed as enterprise: a product, a workplace, a nonprofit, a marketplace. The crime, when there is one, does not look like street crime. Edwin Sutherland named that problem in *White Collar Crime* (1949): wrongdoing inside occupations that the public treats as respectable.

Two US Federal Trade Commission guides are practical benchmarks. They are not the legal standard for every country. They are a clear statement of what a substantiated claim looks like.

- [Green Guides, summarized](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/environmental-claims-summary-green-guides). Broad environmental claims need evidence. “Sustainable,” “recycled,” and “impact” are claims, not moods.
- [Business guidance on multi-level marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing). Earnings claims should show typical net results after expenses. The analogy holds for any pitch that sells a livelihood. It does not mean the organization is a multi-level scheme.

Staw’s escalation paper, above, belongs here as well. People keep paying into a failing course of action because stopping would mean the earlier payments were a loss.

## 6. Who holds the power: the Gervais Principle

Venkatesh Rao’s *The Gervais Principle* is a series of essays on hierarchy, using *The Office* and a cartoon by Hugh MacLeod. It is satire and organizational theory. It is not a test for clinical psychopathy. Rao is explicit that his “sociopath” is not a diagnosis and not a synonym for evil.

The hierarchy has three roles:

- **Sociopaths**, in Rao’s sense, hold strategy and extract value. They see the game.
- **Clueless** supply loyalty, ideology, and bureaucracy. They believe the official story and enforce it.
- **Losers**, again his word, do the productive work and make the economic compromises. They keep the place running.

The essay that matters for money and credit is [Part V, “Heads I Win, Tails You Lose”](https://ribbonfarm.com/2011/10/14/the-gervais-principle-v-heads-i-win-tails-you-lose/). People with effective power keep the benefit of success. Other people absorb the cost of failure.

What to look for:

- A brand, a platform, or a marketplace that captures work done by people who do not control it.
- A middle layer that enforces rules against the people doing the work.
- Engineers, fabricators, and volunteers supplying the knowledge while credit and income sit elsewhere.
- A public story about scale, impact, or technical success that the shop floor does not match.
- Risk left with the builder: equipment, safety, debt. Reputation and sales left with the promoter.
- Critics removed, and the failure handed back to them as their own incompetence.

Start here:

- [Part I — The Office According to The Office](https://ribbonfarm.com/2009/10/07/the-gervais-principle-or-the-office-according-to-the-office/)
- [Part V — Heads I Win, Tails You Lose](https://ribbonfarm.com/2011/10/14/the-gervais-principle-v-heads-i-win-tails-you-lose/)
- [Morality, Compassion and the Sociopath](https://ribbonfarm.com/2009/11/21/morality-compassion-and-the-sociopath/)

## 7. Reporting and further reading

Journalism is not a substitute for the research above. It is a way to watch the same mechanisms in cases that were reported at length.

- [How People Leave One Cult — and End Up in Another](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/what-is-cult-hopping-nxivm-dos-838750/), EJ Dickson, *Rolling Stone*, May 2019. Recruitment, dependency, and why leaving one closed group can lead straight into the next.
- [Was He Really Leading a Cult?](https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/r-kelly-was-he-really-leading-a-cult-1903/), Elizabeth Yuko, *Rolling Stone*, July 2017. Specialists, including Steven Hassan, on isolation, coercive control, and manipulation.
- [If MAGA Is a Cult, What Happens When It Crumbles?](https://au.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/maga-cult-donald-trump-95659/), Elizabeth Yuko, *Rolling Stone*, May 2026. Personality cults, leader loyalty, and what group identity does after the leader fails.
- [Cults archive](https://au.rollingstone.com/t/cults/), *Rolling Stone*. Longer investigations, including the Rajneesh movement, The Family, and Divine Truth.

## There will be light

The mechanisms above are old. Charismatic authority, a closed story, social exclusion, and the use of an idealistic community are documented across decades. They work best where a small group owns the information, the recognition, and the way in.

Those conditions are changing. People can now keep a copy of the work, publish outside the official channel, compare measurements, and talk to each other without asking the founder. Manipulation is still here. What is weaker is the monopoly on information, credit, and access to a community.

The cultural name for this hope is the Age of Aquarius: a turn from hierarchy, charismatic authority, and concentrated knowledge toward distributed intelligence, cooperation, and individual autonomy. Treat that as an aspiration with a history, not as an astrological fact. The 1960s counterculture used the phrase for a more equal and more conscious society. The part that can be built is practical. Shared files, independent hosting, and the freedom to fork are infrastructure for an old wish: knowledge that is not owned by one office, authority that can be checked, cooperation that does not require dependency, and disagreement that is allowed to stand.

### Open networks

**The Wealth of Networks**
Yochai Benkler, 2006.
[Full book, PDF](https://www.benkler.org/Benkler_Wealth_Of_Networks.pdf)

How people collaborate, invent, and produce knowledge on open networks without handing the knowledge, or the right to take part, to one organization. Cooperation no longer needs a single owner.

**Peer Production, the Commons, and the Future of the Firm**
Yochai Benkler, 2016. *Strategic Organization*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1476127016652606)

What commons-based production has already done, and what that does to firms that assume they must own the work. In open hardware and software, contributors can keep a copy, fork it, and maintain it after the original project turns on them.

**The Wisdom of Crowds**
James Surowiecki, 2004.

Diverse and sufficiently independent groups can outperform a single expert, when the group has a way to combine what people actually know. Decentralization, diversity, and independent judgment are the conditions. A room that repeats one voice is not a crowd in this sense.

**The Dawn of Everything**
David Graeber and David Wengrow, 2021.

A challenge to the idea that human societies must harden into one permanent hierarchy. Some of the history is debated. The useful point is narrower: authoritarian organization is one arrangement people have used, not the only one available.

### What the group needs instead of a leader who cannot be questioned

**Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups**
Anita Williams Woolley and others, 2010. *Science*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147)

Across 699 people, group performance tracked social sensitivity and how evenly people spoke. It did not track having the smartest person in charge.

**Collective Intelligence in the Organization of Science**
Anita Williams Woolley and Helge Fuchs, 2011. *Organization Science*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1110.0648)

Healthy knowledge communities depend on divergent views, reflection, and disagreement that is allowed to do work. A room that treats disagreement as disloyalty is giving up the thing that makes a group intelligent.

**Accountability through (Inter)Action?**
Cecilia Gullberg and Nils Gustafsson, 2025. *Social Media + Society*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051251340146)

A public platform does not by itself produce accountability. Visibility and interaction are different things. A large audience is not a community, and a feed is not an audit.

### What is actually changing

The 2025 Edelman Trust Barometer, a survey across 28 countries, reports that 61% hold a moderate or high grievance against institutions, and 36% believe the next generation will be better off.
[Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/trust/2025/trust-barometer)

Those numbers describe grievance and pessimism. They do not prove that people are getting harder to manipulate. What has changed is that alternatives exist:

| Older pattern | Alternative that now exists |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge held in one place | Open repositories and copies people can keep |
| Authority from reputation | Work you can inspect, and results you can repeat |
| One platform as the only door | Independent hosting, federation, and forks |
| Loyalty to founders | Maintainers who can be replaced, and rules that are written down |
| Dissent deleted | Independent publication and a public record |
| A claim you are asked to feel | A measurement you can check |

The tools are real. Their use is a choice. The same networks also spread misinformation, harassment, charismatic influence, and coordinated manipulation. A feed can rebuild the closed room at a larger scale.

### The measure

Open-source software, commons-based production, and independent publishing have already shown that serious work does not require one owner of the knowledge or a leader who cannot be wrong. Collective-intelligence research points the same way: cooperation depends on people being able to hear each other, not on one person being believed.

Environmental and open-technology work is judged on the quality of the work, the treatment of the people who did it, and results that can be measured. Follower counts, founder status, and the repetition of a mission are not those results.

Collaborative technology does not need another movement that claims to hold the solution. It needs practitioners who can do the job, knowledge that stays open, institutions that can be checked, results an outsider can verify, and room to disagree.

**The measure of progress is not how many people believe in a movement, but how many people can act, learn, and build without depending on it.**

## Technology: the declining cost of checking

Artificial intelligence changes the price of information control. Work that used to need a specialist, an expensive program, an institutional archive, or weeks of digging can now be started by one person with an assistant. That matters for any organization whose standing depends on holding the knowledge, keeping a smooth public story, or making independent checking feel too hard to bother with.

AI makes checking cheaper and more widely available. It does not yet guarantee that a falsehood is caught before it spreads.

### Research

**Durably Reducing Conspiracy Beliefs Through Dialogues with AI**
Thomas H. Costello, Gordon Pennycook, and David G. Rand, 2024. *Science*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814)

In an experiment with 2,190 people, a personalized conversation with an AI reduced belief in conspiracy theories by about 20%. The effect was still there two months later. People can reconsider a strongly held belief when the counterevidence is specific to what they actually think. The paper later received a 2026 expression of concern and a correction. Read those before treating the percentage as settled.

**Dialogues with Large Language Models Reduce Conspiracy Beliefs Even When the AI Is Perceived as Human**
Estelle Boissin and others, 2025. *PNAS Nexus*.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf325)

A preregistered experiment with 955 people found that an evidence-based dialogue lowered confidence in unsupported beliefs whether the other side was presented as a human expert or as an AI. The persuasion came from the evidence the person could reach, not from the status of the speaker.

**Community-Based Fact-Checking Reduces the Spread of Misleading Posts on X**
Yuwei Chuai and others, 2026. *Nature Communications*.
[Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72597-0)

Across 237,180 fact-checked repost cascades, community notes cut later reposts of misleading posts by an estimated 61.2% once a note was on screen. Notes often arrived after the most viral stretch, so the drop in total repost engagement was smaller: 14.9%. The median wait from the original post to a displayed note was 18.1 hours. Distributed checking works. Speed decides how much of the audience it still reaches.

**Decentralization and AI**
Linux Foundation Research.
[Report](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/research/decentralized-internet)

Decentralized infrastructure, open-source development, AI agents, and digital ownership as alternatives to a platform one company operates. A community can keep its own infrastructure instead of renting the only door from a proprietor.

**Artificial Intelligence Markets**
OECD, 2026.
[Report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/artificial-intelligence-markets_d531d73f-en/full-report.html)

AI can reopen markets. It can also build new gatekeepers through proprietary data, cloud infrastructure, chips, and distribution. Older keepers of the archive can lose their grip while a new set forms around the model and the machines that run it.

Two later reviews keep the optimism honest:

- [Professional and community fact-checking](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/professional-and-community-based-fact-checking-show-different-strengths-but-neither-performs-strongly-across-trust-scalability-and-impact/), Harvard Kennedy School Misinformation Review, July 2026. A review of 21 studies. Neither professional checkers nor community checkers are strong on trust, scale, and impact at once. Hybrid systems, community participation plus professional verification, do better than either alone.
- [Prior beliefs and automated fact-checking](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0342332), *PLOS ONE*, February 2026. What someone already believes, and whether they trust the AI, still decides whether an automated correction lands.

### What changes in the work

The shift is wider than fact-checking. It touches the tools a closed group uses to stay closed.

| Old advantage | What an individual can do now |
| --- | --- |
| Expertise held by the insiders | Assisted review of engineering, code, and documents |
| A private library | Search across archives the group does not own |
| Authority from reputation | Compare claims, designs, prices, and measured performance |
| Investigation only the funded can afford | Extract, cross-reference, and look for what does not add up |
| Contributors censored on the official channel | Publish elsewhere, carry the tools, host the record |
| One proprietary community platform | Open protocols, federated systems, assisted migration |
| An environmental slogan | Compare it with engineering data, standards, and a lifecycle account |

A newcomer looking at a €10,000 machine no longer has only the seller’s explanation. An assisted review can compare specifications, read a bill of materials, notice missing safety documents, estimate what production would actually cost, and set the offer next to industrial machines that already do the job. That first pass is not a professional engineering validation. It is the round of scrutiny that used to be too expensive to start.

### Before the claim spreads

Chuai and others, above, measured both sides of the same system: 61.2% fewer later reposts once a note is visible, and 18.1 hours as the median wait before that note appears. Most of the travel happens in the wait.

The interval between publication and a check can shrink from hours or days toward seconds or minutes. Systems already process narrow, domain-specific claims quickly. One proof of concept reported under seven seconds. That result does not show reliable performance on general public claims.

Three stages, not three promises:

- **Now.** A person can question, search, and investigate a claim they have in front of them.
- **Next.** Agents can watch assertions, fetch independent evidence, and flag what is unsupported.
- **Possible.** Checking becomes part of how information moves, instead of a correction published after the damage.

AI is also a source of the problem. In a 2025 international study coordinated by the European Broadcasting Union, 45% of evaluated AI-generated news answers contained at least one significant issue. An assistant is not an authority on what is true. Its useful job is to make source-based checking easier: evidence someone else can reproduce, uncertainty stated in the open, and a person still responsible for the conclusion.

### What this does to institutional advantage

Organizations have long held an informational lead over their audience. Judging a design, comparing products, reading a financial claim, or reconstructing a history took time, specialist knowledge, and documents most people could not get. Those barriers are lower.

Claims of technical superiority can be set against measured performance. Environmental promises can be set against material balances, energy use, and a lifecycle assessment. Claims of years of development can be set against public repositories, contributor histories, and the designs that were actually published. A commercial promise can be set against competing products, real production costs, and operating conditions a shop can survive.

Personalized, evidence-based dialogue can help people reconsider an unsupported belief. Timely community correction can cut how far a misleading post then travels. Early intervention at a universal scale has not been shown. Automated retrieval is a way to shorten the wait, not a finished guarantee.

Artificial intelligence is not automatically decentralized, reliable, or safe from manipulation. It can invent, repeat a bias, and concentrate power in the companies that own the computers, the models, and the data. The useful combination is open standards, sources an outsider can reach, methods that are written down, infrastructure that is not one company’s, and a human who remains accountable.

An organization can still publish a misleading claim. It can still delete criticism from its own channels, and it can still take credit for work other people did. It no longer has to be the only holder of the evidence, the only one who can investigate, or the only route by which contributors speak to each other.

**The shift is from authority that depends on controlling the story to credibility earned by results someone else can check.**

That shift is unfinished, and it is not guaranteed. Deception has not become obsolete. What is available now is the means to check, and a reason for a community to keep its own copies.
