# Documentation Archive: Investigation into the Structural and Legal Collapse of Precious Plastic

## 1. Overview of the Initiative and Central Conflict

Precious Plastic was established in 2013 by Dave Hakkens as an open-source global recycling initiative, providing free blueprints for small-scale plastic recycling machinery (shredders, extruders, injectors). Over time, the ecosystem fractured into severe internal community disputes, operational insolvency, and legal battles regarding the economic viability and safety of grassroots plastic recycling.

---

## 2. Macro-Level Context: The Feasibility of Plastic Recycling

Investigative reporting and environmental research (including investigations by NPR and PBS Frontline) indicate that the global plastics industry was aware by the 1970s and 1980s that widespread plastic recycling was largely economically and chemically unfeasible. The "chasing arrows" recycling symbol was utilized primarily as a marketing mechanism to shift moral responsibility onto consumers and avert regulatory bans. Globally, less than 10% of plastic waste is successfully recycled, with the remainder routed to landfills, incineration, or developing nations.

A multi-year digital and legal conflict occurred between the central Precious Plastic organization (operating under the legal umbrella entity One Army) and OSR-Plastic, an alternative digital repository and shop distributing hardware files and components outside the official platform.

## 4. Documented Technical and Operational Failures

## Metric Inflation and Fabricated Impact Data

Public audits and community tracking revealed that a vast percentage of the thousands of active "workspaces" displayed on Precious Plastic’s global marketing map were defunct or empty ghost accounts. Critics argue these metrics were intentionally maintained to artificially inflate the project's global footprint to secure corporate sponsorships, grants, and public donations.

## Toxic Chemical Exposure and Workplace Negligence

The central organization admitted to the closure of its primary workspace due to structural contamination from Hexavalent Chromium (Chrome-6), a severe carcinogen resulting from machine processing and building decay. Independent documentation alleges the core leadership downplayed these hazards and delayed notifying global volunteers to protect the organization's reputation.

## Financial Mismanagement and Capital Diversion

Technical community autopsies (including reviews on platforms such as Hacker News) highlighted severe cash-flow mismanagement. The central organization publicly confirmed receiving a €100,000 lump-sum donation and immediately distributing it via community grants to unverified third parties rather than stabilizing its own cash-strapped operations. Following subsequent financial strain, the entity panic-sold its entire industrial tool asset base below cost.

## The Consultation Agreement

To secure operational revenue, the central organization entered into a commercial consultation agreement ("Collab") with a major cosmetics corporation in Manhattan, New York, to build a local recycling facility. The workspace was outfitted with DIY-style machinery sourced from a community fabricator and operated by non-specialized local workers.

## The Industrial Accident

A severe physical injury occurred involving an operator running the machinery. Technical reviews of the baseline machine blueprints revealed critical design defects according to commercial factory standards:



* Lack of Guarding: Open-hopper configurations that allow direct access to high-torque shredding components without elongated infeed chutes.

* Electrical Non-Compliance: Entirely lacking basic industrial safety mechanisms, such as mandatory physical finger guards, emergency-stop grounding, or automatic electrical start/stop interlocks.



## Legal and Financial Fallout

Under New York product liability law, the design of a product that presents a substantial likelihood of harm when a safer alternative design is feasible subjects the designer to strict liability.



* A personal injury lawsuit was filed in New York targeting the cosmetics corporation, the community fabricator, and the central organization (operating legally as the One Army Entity).

* To manage the multi-year litigation, discovery requests, and depositions, the uninsured organization was forced to retain New York defense council at high hourly rates.

* The accumulation of these legal fees drained the entity's remaining cash reserves, halting platform software development and resulting in organizational insolvency.

## 6. Conclusion and Advisory: Impact on the Community, Investors, and Open-Source Ecosystem

The trajectory of the central Precious Plastic entity serves as a definitive case study in the structural distortion of grassroots environmental movements. When evaluating the broader impacts across participants, backers, and open-source methodology, several systemic harms are identified:

## Impact on the Grassroots Community and Volunteers


* Exploitation and Free-Loading: The central organization leveraged unpaid volunteer labor, crowd-sourced translations, and community moderation under the guise of an egalitarian movement. This goodwill was subsequently converted into commercial consulting portfolios ("Collabs") and monopolized marketplace structures ("The Bazar") without financial equity or compensation for the creators involved.

* Credit and Intellectual Property Theft: Independent modifications, technical safety upgrades, and heavy-duty alterations engineered by grassroots builders were routinely copied, integrated, and rebranded by central leadership as official internal developments (e.g., V4 and Pro lines), structurally erasing the attribution rights fundamental to open-source protocols.

* Digital Enforcement Over Youth Factions: Platform administration utilized heavy-handed moderation, public blacklisting campaigns, and targeted online exclusion against younger, independent makers who questioned the central financial structure, technical efficacy, or safety profiles of the official blueprints.



## Impact on Investors and Financial Donors

* Fabricated Metrics and Deceptive Funding Pools: By deliberately maintaining thousands of non-operational, inactive, or completely fabricated ghost accounts on public "Impact Maps," the organization misrepresented its actual operational scale. This data fabrication misled global philanthropic institutions, grant bodies, and private crowd-funders into investing capital under false pretenses.

* Zero-Outcome Capital Allocation: Donors were repeatedly solicited for emergency capital and platform expansions (such as the unvouched "V5" iteration) without a verifiable operational roadmap. This lack of oversight led to systemic mismanagement, including the absolute depletion of a six-figure (€100,000) lump-sum corporate donation while the primary infrastructure simultaneously faced structural insolvency.

## Structural Harm to the Open-Source Ecosystem


* Weaponized Gatekeeping: The transition from decentralized blueprint distribution to a centralized commercial marketplace demonstrated how a foundational open-source brand can be leveraged to extort independent fabricators, force platform fee compliance, and choke off autonomous distribution channels.

* The Liability Chasm: By pushing uncertified, non-OSHA/CE/UL compliant physical hardware schematics as accessible "DIY backyard projects," the entity offloaded immense legal and physical liabilities onto individual makers, schools, and spaces. The resultant physical trauma injuries and catastrophic product liability litigation (e.g., the New York Manhattan litigation) highlight how a lack of strict engineering compliance within open-source designs can trigger complete organizational and ecosystem ruin.

## 1. Deceptive Metrics and Capital Solicitation (Prospect/Data Fraud)

Soliciting donations, corporate grants, or consulting contracts by intentionally presenting fabricated impact metrics (such as keeping thousands of defunct or ghost accounts active on an ecosystem map to inflate operational size) violates major federal white-collar statutes.

### Federal Law:

* Wire Fraud ([18 U.S. Code § 1343](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1343)): This is the primary federal tool for digital fraud. It applies whenever a "scheme or artifice to defraud" or to obtain money through "false or fraudulent pretenses, representations, or promises" is transmitted via interstate or international electronic communication (the internet, emails, payment gateways, or web platforms). Convictions carry up to 20 years in federal prison per count.
* Mail Fraud ([18 U.S. Code § 1341](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1341)): Similar to wire fraud, this applies if the US Postal Service or private interstate commercial carriers (e.g., FedEx, UPS) were used at any point to transport contracts, marketing materials, or fabricated financial documentation to secure US funding. [1] 

### State Law (New York):

* Scheme to Defraud (N.Y. Penal Law § 190.60 / 190.65): In New York, engaging in a systematic ongoing course of conduct to defraud ten or more persons, or obtaining property from more than one person via false pretenses, constitutes a criminal felony.
